# Learning Action Priors for Cross-embodiment Robot Manipulation

- **arXiv:** [2606.26095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26095) (24 Jun 2026)
- **Authors:** Dong Jing, Tianqi Zhang, Jiaqi Liu, Jinman Zhao, Zelong Sun, Li Erran Li, Zhiwu Lu, Mingyu Ding
- **Affiliations:** Renmin University of China · UNC Chapel Hill · University of Toronto · Amazon
- **Status:** abstract read, full paper NOT yet read

## Problem it attacks

A VLA inherits strong visual and linguistic priors from its VLM backbone, but the **action module starts from scratch**. Early optimisation therefore has to discover temporal action dynamics *and* cross-modal alignment at the same time — and that gets worse in cross-embodiment settings, where action distributions are heterogeneous.

## Method — two stages

**Stage 1.** A lightweight **flow-matching encoder–decoder** action module learns temporal motion structure from **unconditioned action trajectories only** — no visual or language tokens processed at all.

**Stage 2.** The prior transfers into VLA training by (a) **decoder reuse** and (b) **early-stage latent distillation**, aligning vision-language features to the action embedding space while still permitting end-to-end refinement.

Bonus: the Stage-1 encoder doubles as a **history compressor**, squeezing state-action history into a single temporal context token.

## Claimed results

13 cross-embodiment tasks, sim + real. Versus VLA-without-action-priors: faster convergence, higher success rate, and "substantially stronger" results on **data-scarce real-world tasks**. Scaling Stage-1 action data yields a more generalisable prior that improves downstream VLA performance.

⚠️ The abstract does not enumerate the embodiments, their DoF/end-effector heterogeneity, the baselines beyond "no action prior", or any numbers. **Read the paper before citing any of that.**

## Why it matters for ZERO

Directly on our problem — but the useful part is a specific, cheap idea:

1. **Stage 1 needs actions only.** No images, no language, no reward. Our EEF-space action stream (`2 × (pos3 + rot6d + grip1)`) qualifies, and so does *any* other EEF trajectory data we can find. That means we can pretrain an action prior on trajectories from **both** the reBot and the G1 — and from unrelated public datasets in the same representation — before we have a single good demo of our own task.

2. **It targets exactly our weakest axis: data scarcity.** The plan starts at ~50 episodes. "Substantially stronger performance on data-scarce real-world tasks" is the claim most relevant to us, and the one to verify first when reading the full paper.

3. **Flow matching is already the family we know.** The Stage-1 module is a flow-matching encoder-decoder, which is the architecture used in the previous project — so this is a re-sequencing of training, not a new model class to learn.

4. **Caution — it is a *latent* action prior, not a shared action space.** This does not solve reBot(6-DoF + parallel jaw) → G1(7-DoF + Dex3) on its own. Our transfer mechanism is still the explicit shared EEF representation plus per-robot IK. Action priors are an *optimisation* aid layered on top, not a substitute. Do not conflate the two.

## Open questions to answer from the full paper

- What action representation do their heterogeneous embodiments share? If they use raw joint vectors of differing dimension, how is that padded/masked — and does that beat an explicit EEF space like ours?
- How much Stage-1 data before the prior helps? Is there a floor below which it does nothing?
- Does the history-compressor token cause the copycat/causal-confusion failure that long proprioceptive history is known for?
- Sim-to-sim or real only? Our whole plan is sim→sim, so their evaluation setting matters for how much to trust the transfer numbers.
